Fig. 1  (fig-selection-scaling)  --  Cost of one gate selection
---------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{Cost of one gate selection.} Time to select a single gate -- pair enumeration,
the occupation-flip table and scoring, the three phases timed inside the engine --
against the mode count $N$, for 3 generator/order cases in the propagated basis, single
partition, single thread. Building and evolving the observable happen before the clock
starts. Fitted log-log slopes over the 4 widest rungs of each case: $\ell=2$, $p=1$:
$N^{3.07}$ against the $N^{3}$ bound, over $N=96$--192; $\ell=2$, $p=2$: $N^{4.22}$
against the $N^{4}$ bound, over $N=64$--160; $\ell=3$, $p=2$: $N^{5.19}$ against the
$N^{5}$ bound, over $N=32$--80. Each dotted line is the corresponding $N^{\ell+p}$
pair-count bound, anchored on the widest measured rung. The measured time exponents
track the fitted pair-count exponents (3.02), (4.06), (5.19) to within 0.16, and the
cost of enumerating one pair stays flat at 9--16~ns across the whole sweep, so selection
costs what enumerating the pairs costs and carries no hidden work on top of it.
