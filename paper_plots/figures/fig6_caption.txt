Fig. 6 -- The marginal cost of a gate, single-threaded.

Model: kicked-Ising chain of N qubits, open boundary; 7 layers, each Rzz(pi/4) on all N-1
nearest-neighbour bonds then Rx(pi/4) on all N sites, so a layer holds 2N-1 gates; observable
sum_i Z_i propagated in the Heisenberg picture (the layer order therefore reverses);
truncation a Pauli-weight cutoff of 6 with lower_atol = 0, so no coefficient pruning at all
and the surviving term set is fixed by gate supports alone. N = 32..1024 in powers of two
(monoprop 6, ppvm 6, PauliPropagation.jl 5 points), one thread, every work item an exclusive
job on one of 10 nodes of one instance type.

Plotted: wall-clock nanoseconds for ONE gate to act on ONE term, against N. Both divisors
are checked, not assumed: at every point all three engines report the same term count
(607,054 at N=32 rising to 25,254,286 at N=1024), the same gate count (441 to 14329) and an
expectation value agreeing to 8.8e-14 absolute, so the divisors are identical across the
three curves and the figure script refuses to build if any of that moves. A shared divisor
cannot manufacture a difference between the curves, only reveal one.

Result. monoprop falls as N^-0.80, close to the ideal 1/N drawn beside it: 1.352 ns at
N=32 down to 0.0833 ns at N=1024, a 16.2x reduction across a 32x wider system.
Neither reference engine falls: ppvm N^+0.39, PauliPropagation.jl N^+0.41.
Note where they start -- at N=32 monoprop leads ppvm by only 1.5x and PauliPropagation.jl by
1.8x, against 41x and 50x by N=512, so the gap is opened over the sweep and not assumed at the
origin.

The fit window. Every exponent above is fitted over N <= 512, the one window every engine was
measured across; the points beyond it are drawn but enter no fit. PauliPropagation.jl stops at
N=512: its N=1024 point was not measured, because past the packed-key cliff described next
that single point runs for hours, and above N = 512 it could not enter a like-for-like fit in
any case. Nothing on the canvas says even that much -- the legend carries engine names only --
so this caption is the record.

Beyond N = 512 PauliPropagation.jl crosses a packed-key width boundary, measured at
N=576->608: an 8.5x single-N jump in the v0.8.2 full-width probe and 7.7x in its
idle-spectator sweep (8.7x and 7.1x at v0.7.3, so the vectorised backend does not move it).
ppvm's largest single-N step in these rows is 1.50x, at N=128->256. A power law fitted across
a discontinuity partly measures where the step falls rather than per-term cost in N, and that
step is a fixed-width-integer artifact which Figs. 2 and 3 already own. Fitted over every
point each engine reached instead, the same rows give monoprop N^-0.80, ppvm N^+0.39 and
PauliPropagation.jl N^+0.41. monoprop's own exponent is the same to two decimals (-0.80),
having no such boundary to cross. At N=1024 monoprop leads ppvm by 92x; at N=512, the widest
point all three reached, it leads ppvm by 41x and PauliPropagation.jl by 50x.

The divisor is not the effect. A falling curve invites the objection that the gate count is
itself proportional to N, so state the same rows with no gate division at all: a monoprop
layer of 2047 gates costs 2.00x what a layer of 63 gates costs (85.2 -> 170.5 ms per
10^6 terms). 32.5x the gates for 2.00x the time. The identical divisor applied to the
other two engines leaves them flat or rising, so it cannot be the source of the fall.

Nor is the cutoff. This panel draws one arm, so the cutoff-independence comes from the
companion step-32 sweep over N=32..512 (16 points, all three cutoffs; 5 layers, on
Aaron-MacbookAir-Work.local), fitted over the same N <= 512 window:
    cutoff K (N=32->512)          monoprop    QuEra ppvm  PauliPropagation.jl
    2      188 -> 3,068           N^-0.96     N^-0.06     N^+0.19
    4      10,122 -> 183,882      N^-0.81     N^+0.31     N^+0.24
    6      119,280 -> 2,310,000   N^-0.86     N^+0.58     N^+0.56
monoprop falls at all three and neither reference engine is ever below N^-0.06, so the
choice of arm is not load-bearing; cutoff 6 is drawn for continuity with Figs. 1-5.

Mechanism: selectivity, not batching. The operator is held transposed -- one column per bit
position, bit r set iff term r touches that position
(cpp/monoprop/detail/operator/InvertedIndex.h). Each column sits in one of two tiers, and
below a density of 1/64 it is an ascending set-row list rather than a full-height
bit-vector, with combine_columns_block narrowing even a dense column to a word range. A
gate on qubit i therefore costs work proportional to the terms that actually touch qubit i,
which at a fixed weight cutoff is a ~w/N fraction of the operator, rather than a scan over
all K terms. One fact, both readings: per gate the cost carries the 1/N, and per layer each
of the 2N-1 gates takes its ~1/N slice, so the layer total barely moves. The reference
engines hold a packed key per term and revisit the whole sum for every gate, so nothing in
them can fall.

Why to believe it, from the companion sweep (Fig. 5a; 5 layers, on
Aaron-MacbookAir-Work.local). Confining the whole model to a 32-qubit active window and
padding the register out to N with idle spectator qubits holds the term count (119,280), the
gate count (315), every term's support and the expectation value EXACTLY fixed while only the
register width changes. Over a 32x widening monoprop pays 1.6x, ppvm 8.3x and
PauliPropagation.jl 146x. That is per-term N-overhead isolated from everything else, and the
same ordering as here.

Caveats, stated because the figure would otherwise overclaim:

  * WHERE. Measured on the AWS PCS cluster, not on an exclusive Leonardo node: 10 nodes of one
    instance type (AWS m7a.8xlarge, AMD EPYC 9R14 (Zen 4), SMT off). The sweep ran as
    independent Slurm jobs, each holding a whole node exclusively with the timed process
    pinned to one core, so that no single point could hold the rest hostage; that is why the
    rows record 10 hostnames. That the split does not move the result is measured, not
    assumed: a rerun of the same workload for monoprop and ppvm with every point on one node
    (ip-10-0-0-165) agrees point by point to within 6.8% (the worst is ppvm at N=512), and
    shifts no exponent by more than 0.009 over N=32..1024, or 0.023 over N <= 512, where the
    fit's endpoint levers the slope hardest. The dataset is still cited only for a SHAPE (an
    exponent in N) and never for an absolute time. Figs. 1-4 are the absolute-time figures and
    they come from Leonardo.
  * Timed repetitions differ by engine, and the rows record it: monoprop 10, QuEra ppvm 1,
    PauliPropagation.jl 1. monoprop's points run 0.36 to 30 s, short enough at the low end
    that timer granularity and process noise matter, so its minimum is taken over many more
    rounds; the reference engines' points run for up to 46 minutes each, where one round
    already resolves them.
  * num_terms is the FINAL term count, while K grows through the 7 layers, so the absolute
    ns per term understates the true per-term cost. It understates it identically for all
    three engines, so the comparison between the curves is unaffected.
  * N=1024 is the ceiling, not a choice: monoprop_MAX_NUM_MODES defaults to 1024 with no
    headroom above it, so a wider sweep needs a rebuild.
  * monoprop's residual against the ideal N^-1 is real work, not noise: the emit path still
    materialises the dense partner and folds every word for the hash, which is O(N/32) work
    on the branching fraction. The 1/N is approached, not attained.
