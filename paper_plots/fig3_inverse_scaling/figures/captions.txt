Fig. 3 -- Inverse time scaling with qubit number.

Model: kicked-Ising chain of N qubits, open boundary; 7 layers, each Rzz(pi/4) on all N-1
nearest-neighbour bonds then Rx(pi/4) on all N sites, so a layer holds 2N-1 gates; observable
sum_i Z_i propagated in the Heisenberg picture (the layer order therefore reverses);
truncation a Pauli-weight cutoff of 6 with lower_atol = 0, so no coefficient pruning at all
and the surviving term set is fixed by gate supports alone. N = 32..1024 in powers of two
(monoprop 6, ppvm 6, PauliPropagation.jl 5 points), one thread, every series on one exclusive
node (cnx001.deucalion.macc.fccn.pt).

Plotted: wall-clock nanoseconds for ONE gate to act on ONE term, against N. Both divisors are
checked, not assumed: at every point all engines report the same term count (607,054 at N=32
rising to 25,254,286 at N=1024), the same gate count (441 to 14329) and an expectation value
agreeing to 8.8e-14 absolute, so the divisors are identical across the curves and the figure
script refuses to build if any of that moves.

Result. monoprop falls as N^-0.93, close to the ideal 1/N drawn beside it: 1.819 ns at N=32
down to 0.0700 ns at N=1024, a 26.0x reduction across a 32x wider system. Neither reference
engine falls: ppvm N^+0.30, PauliPropagation.jl N^+0.35. At N=32 monoprop leads ppvm by only
1.8x and PauliPropagation.jl by 2.2x, against 54x and 73x by N=512, so the gap is opened over
the sweep and not assumed at the origin. At N=1024 monoprop leads ppvm by 158x.

The fit window. Every exponent above, and in the legend, is fitted over N <= 512, the widest
window all three engines completed; the points beyond it are drawn but enter no fit.
PauliPropagation.jl stops at N=512: its N=1024 point exceeded the 3600 s time limit and is
absent. Fitted over every point each engine reached instead, the same rows give monoprop
N^-0.94, ppvm N^+0.35 and PauliPropagation.jl N^+0.35. ppvm's largest single-N step in these
rows is 1.45x, at N=512->1024.

The divisor is not the effect. A falling curve invites the objection that the gate count is
itself proportional to N, so state the same rows with no gate division at all: a monoprop
layer of 2047 gates costs 1.25x what a layer of 63 gates costs (114.6 -> 143.3 ms per 10^6
terms). 32.5x the gates for 1.25x the time. The identical divisor applied to the other two
engines leaves them flat or rising, so it cannot be the source of the fall.

Mechanism: selectivity, not batching. The operator is held transposed -- one column per bit
position, bit r set iff term r touches that position
(cpp/monoprop/detail/operator/InvertedIndex.h). Below a density of 1/64 a column is an
ascending set-row list rather than a full-height bit-vector. A gate on qubit i therefore costs
work proportional to the terms that touch qubit i, which at a fixed weight cutoff is a ~w/N
fraction of the operator, rather than a scan over all K terms. The reference engines hold a
packed key per term and revisit the whole sum for every gate, so nothing in them can fall.

Caveats, stated because the figure would otherwise overclaim:

  * WHERE. Measured on Deucalion's x86 partition (AMD EPYC 7742 (Zen 2), 128 cores, SMT off),
    not on Leonardo. Every series was measured in one Slurm job on one node, held exclusively,
    with the timed process pinned to one core, so every curve -- and every fitted exponent --
    comes from the same node. The dataset is cited for a SHAPE (an exponent in N), never for
    an absolute time.
  * Timed repetitions differ by engine, and the rows record it: monoprop 10, ppvm 1,
    PauliPropagation.jl 1. monoprop's points run 0.49 to 25 s, short enough at the low end
    that timer granularity and process noise matter, so its minimum is taken over many more
    rounds; the reference engines' points run for up to 67 minutes each, where one round
    already resolves them.
  * num_terms is the FINAL term count, while K grows through the 7 layers, so the absolute ns
    per term understates the true per-term cost. It understates it identically for all three
    engines, so the comparison between the curves is unaffected.
  * N=1024 is the ceiling, not a choice: monoprop_MAX_NUM_MODES defaults to 1024 with no
    headroom above it, so a wider sweep needs a rebuild.
  * monoprop's residual against the ideal N^-1 is measured, not assumed: at N=1024 the curve
    sits 1.23x above the 1/N guide through its N=32 point, while its last two octaves fall as
    N^-0.98 and N^-1.00; per-term instruction counts are flat in N, so the gap is accrued
    below N=256 as one-time steps rather than a slope: there the operator outgrows a 16 MiB L3
    slice (simulated last-level misses per anticommuting term rise 0.63 -> 2.33 from N=32 to
    N=256) and, above N=128, stored term positions widen from 8 to 16 bits. The 1/N is
    approached, not attained.
